**Asset:** Tecolutla field, Contractual Area 24, Tampico-Misantla Basin, Veracruz, Mexico
**Operator of record:** Tonalli Energia (IFR JV)
**Purpose:** Technical handover to incoming operator — transparent basis of review
**Version:** 3 — incorporates full AFE, Simmons economic scenarios, well design detail, and the TEC-11 mud log
**Status:** Synthesis from primary source documents in the International Frontier shared drive. Sources read in full are listed in Section 10. Items flagged **[to verify]** have not been opened.

## 1. Executive summary

**The field.** Tecolutla produces from the Upper El Abra carbonate, a reef-rim build-up on the Golden Lane trend at roughly 2,300 m subsea. Seven PEMEX wells were drilled 1956–1972, four of which produced. Tonalli drilled TEC-10 (directional, successful) and TEC-11 (horizontal, failed) in 2018. Approximately 2 MMbbl recovered against an internally estimated 11.2 MMbbl OOIP — a recovery factor of about 18%.

**The drive mechanism is the central fact.** Reservoir pressure is reported at original 1956 pressure of 24.7 MPa. Strong aquifer support has maintained pressure through 2 MMbbl of offtake. Every well shows the same signature: steady GOR with no late-life increase, and progressive water encroachment until the well reaches an economic water cut, is shut in, and later restarted. The field is not pressure-limited. It is water-limited, which makes structural position above the oil-water contact the dominant control on well performance.

**TEC-12 is a low-risk infill, not an exploration test.** A directional well from the existing TEC-10 pad, TD 2,370 mMD / 2,352.6 mTVD, targeting un-perforated crestal El Abra pay that PEMEX logged and identified but never completed in TEC-6 (-2,294 to -2,307 mSS) and TEC-9 (-2,296 to -2,311 mSS). Offset 175 m from TEC-10, bracketed by wells that produced ~899 mbbl and ~353 mbbl.

**The most important finding of this review: TEC-12 was never uneconomic at the well level. It was uneconomic under IFR's standalone corporate overhead.** In the most-likely Simmons case the field generates $8.59 MM of net operating income over eight years and returns negative $2.57 MM of EBITDA, because $8.32 MM of IFR corporate charge, Tonalli G&A and regulatory cost sits on top of a single-asset company. At the well level, payout ranges from 1.8 to 3.7 years across the central cases. For an acquirer absorbing the asset into an existing Mexico platform, most of that overhead disappears. **This is the honest and compelling explanation for why IFR could not fund the well and why it may still work for the buyer.**

**The TEC-11 failure is now resolved from primary evidence.** The full Rise Energy mud log shows the horizontal lateral ran through roughly 900 m of dominantly mudstone–wackestone with poor to scarce oil impregnation and only intermittent miliolid grainstone stringers. The well reached the correct stratigraphic level and the wrong rock. The completion failure determined how it died, not whether it would. This is exploration risk on a step-out from the TEC-2 pad into undrilled facies — categorically different from TEC-12, which drills logged pay 175 m from the TEC-10 pad between two wells that produced ~899 mbbl and ~353 mbbl. See Section 5.

**One material inconsistency remains to be reconciled before handover:** the TEC-12 production forecast (Section 6.4).

### 2.3 Reservoir and facies

Upper El Abra, described in cuttings as miliolid grainstone and cryptocrystalline limestone. Petrel Robertson identifies three porosity types: intergranular, fracture and karst. Reservoir quality is layered and variable, and hard to quantify on vintage gamma-neutron log control.

**A trap for anyone new to the field:** the TEC-2 micro-log shows production from *high* gamma-ray porous and permeable limestone, while *clean* gamma-ray intervals correspond to tight, thin-bedded miliolid lagoon facies. GR reads opposite to the usual shale-indicator convention here. This should be stated explicitly in the handover.

TEC-10 encountered a high-porosity reef margin in the upper zone, described in the work program as "excellent reservoir."

**Internal volumetrics (Upper El Abra):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Area | 2.5 km² |
| Gross thickness | 42.3 m |
| Net-to-gross | 40% |
| Net pay | 16.9 m |
| Porosity | 7.0% |
| Water saturation | 30% |
| Bo | 1.19 rm³/sm³ |

At a 29% recovery factor (El Abra trend average), roughly **1.2 MMbbl remains recoverable**. Analogue El Abra pools reach up to 45%.

**Discrepancy:** the El Abra trend analogue table in the same deck lists Tecolutla at 3.1 km², 7.8 MMbbl OOIP and 2.0 MMbbl 1P EUR at 25% RF — materially smaller than the 11.2 MMbbl used in the volumetrics. Both appear in the same document. **[to verify]**

### 2.4 Pressure and drive mechanism

Static gradient surveys exist from 1956, 1964, 1971 (a dense TEC-6 series across Aug–Oct), 1973, 1998 and a 2018 extended survey on TEC-2.

Datum pressures at 2,300 m cluster tightly at **23.2–24.7 MPa from 1956 to 2018.**

Implications:
- Very strong aquifer support; effectively no depletion after 2 MMbbl.
- No solution-gas drive decline — steady GOR across every well's life confirms this.
- Gas cap possible but never directly observed; considered unlikely on GOR behaviour.
- Water, not pressure, is the recovery constraint.
- 1964 TEC-6 flow test: 9 m³/d oil, 1.1e3 m³/d gas, 3% water cut.

### 2.5 Type curve

The vertical-well type curve is fitted to TEC-2, TEC-6, TEC-7 and TEC-9 from 1960 onward, described as a good fit, forecasting **345,000 bbl cumulative** per vertical well.

Acknowledged data gaps: nothing before 1960, nothing between 1965 and 1972. Field-level data exists 1960–1965; individual well records begin 1972.

## 3. TEC-10 — the success case and direct analogue

TEC-10 proves modern drilling works at Tecolutla and is the explicit downside case for TEC-12.

- Directional well, 2018. Upper zone encountered high-porosity reef margin.
- Initial post-stimulation flowback: 1,475 bbl oil, improving performance.
- Testing from Sept 2018 showed stable oil cut and high flowing pressures; over 19,000 bbl cumulative at time of reporting.
- Only **~2.5 m perforated**, -2,311.5 to -2,315.0 mSS, producing at ~30% oil cut.
- FMI image log run — recommended to repeat in TEC-12.
- **Came in lower than drilling prognosis**, which triggered the 3D reprocessing and depth migration.

The comparison driving the TEC-12 case: TEC-10 produces commercially from 2.5 m of perforated pay low on structure. TEC-12 targets roughly **15 m of net pay, 16.5 m higher on structure** than the top of the TEC-10 perforations.

## 4. Modern drilling performance — TEC-11 execution record

Documented problems from the Pason performance analysis and depth-time curve:

- Pump failure in the surface hole; deviation correction; slow directional tool make-up.
- Building to 61°; auto-driller not functioning — performance improved once repaired.
- Last 24 hours advanced only 25 m (2,332 → 2,357 mMD) at ~61°.
- Lost time running intermediate casing due to hydraulic arm failure.
- **Liner running problems:** faulty tie-back string, subsequently damaged, fishing for lost pieces, two cement jobs for the liner, hole conditioning.

For context on the contrast with TEC-12: the 8½" section to TD drilled without problems in TEC-9, and the Tantoyuca was open in TEC-11 for many days without issue because the intermediate was set high.

## 5. TEC-11 failure — resolved from the mud log

TEC-11 is why IFR could not fund TEC-12. The file contained two competing accounts. The full Rise Energy mud log (40–3,283 mMD, cuttings at 5 m intervals, dated 4 Dec 2018) resolves them.

### 5.1 What the record said before

**IFR's 2023 work program blamed the completion:**
- Intent was selective acid stimulation with isolated flowback along the horizontal leg.
- The selective stimulation tool could not be run because of the damaged liner hanger.
- Program modified to pump acid from surface without isolating individual perforation intervals.
- Pressure broke down quickly; acid entered at low pressure and high rate.
- Some oil on flowback, transitioning rapidly to 100% water.
- Stated conclusion: acid likely entered a fracture and induced overwhelming aquifer influx.

**The operational understanding was that suitable El Abra was not encountered.** The mud log supports the second reading, and it does so unambiguously.

### 5.2 What the mud log shows

TEC-11 was drilled from the **TEC-2 location**, not the TEC-10 pad. The clastic section runs to about 2,185 m. Carbonate is entered around 2,360 m and the well continues to TD at 3,283 mMD — a lateral of roughly 900 m.

Facies through the reservoir section:

| Interval (mMD) | Dominant lithology | Hydrocarbon shows |
|---|---|---|
| 2,185–2,360 | Bentonitic shale, traces of white mudstone | Poor impregnation from 2,325; light yellow fluorescence |
| 2,360–2,650 | **Mudstone–wackestone**, white/cream/light grey, semi-compact, partly recrystallised, disseminated pyrite, chert traces | Poor to scarce throughout. One packstone with secondary vugular porosity at 2,375–2,380 |
| 2,650–2,750 | Wackestone–**grainstone of miliolids**, compact, partly recrystallised | **None recorded** |
| 2,755–2,810 | **Grainstone** + wackestone, recrystallised, compact | Poor, light yellow fluorescence |
| 2,815–2,915 | Mudstone–wackestone, grainstone of miliolids subordinate | Poor |
| 2,920–2,930 | Wackestone of miliolids, compact, some secondary vugular porosity | **Moderate — yellow-orange fluorescence. Best show in the well.** |
| 2,930–2,965 | Mudstone–wackestone, partly recrystallised | Poor. **Traces of black bituminous oil (gilsonite)** |
| 2,965–3,200 | Mudstone–wackestone, increasingly bentonitic, abundant disseminated pyrite | Poor to scarce |
| 3,200–3,283 | **Bentonite 30–40%**, chalky mudstone, scarce recrystallised wackestone | None |

### 5.3 Interpretation

**TEC-11 did penetrate El Abra. It did not penetrate productive El Abra reservoir.**

Three observations carry the conclusion:

1. **Facies.** The lateral is dominantly mudstone–wackestone — low-energy, tight, back-reef to basinal-margin fabric. Miliolid grainstone appears only as intermittent stringers, roughly 2,650–2,750 and 2,755–2,810, perhaps 150 m of a 900 m lateral. Compare the productive offsets at the same stratigraphic level: TEC-9 at 2,308–2,311 mMD is *cream miliolid grainstone*; TEC-6 at 2,305–2,310 mMD is cryptocrystalline limestone with frequent miliolides.

2. **Saturation.** Oil impregnation is logged as *poor* or *scarce* over essentially the entire carbonate section. Exactly one 10 m interval, 2,920–2,930, records *moderate* impregnation. A well with a 900 m lateral and one moderate show is not in charged reservoir.

3. **Diagenesis and environment.** Abundant disseminated pyrite, persistent bentonite rising to 30–40% near TD, chalky mudstone textures and widespread recrystallisation all point to a low-energy reducing setting — the opposite of the high-energy reef-margin grainstone that produces at Tecolutla. The gilsonite traces at 2,930–2,965 indicate degraded residual hydrocarbon rather than a live column.

**Depth was not the problem; lateral facies was.** The work program places TEC-11 at roughly 2,311+ mSS in the reservoir section, essentially level with the TEC-6 and TEC-9 highest perforations at -2,308 to -2,319 mSS. The well was at the right stratigraphic level and in the wrong rock.

### 5.4 The reconciled account

The two explanations are cause and consequence, not alternatives. The lateral was placed in tight, poorly-saturated mudstone–wackestone for most of its length. The damaged liner hanger then forced an unisolated acid job, which broke down into a fracture and pulled water from the aquifer. **A well in good grainstone might have survived that completion; a well in this facies had nothing to produce regardless.** The root cause is facies placement. The completion failure determined how the well died, not whether it would.

### 5.5 Why this does not transfer to TEC-12

Stated plainly, because it is the crux of the handover:

| | TEC-11 | TEC-12 |
|---|---|---|
| Surface location | TEC-2 pad | TEC-10 pad |
| Geometry | Horizontal, 61° build, ~900 m lateral | Vertical through reservoir, 15° max inclination |
| TD | 3,283 mMD | 2,370 mMD |
| Target definition | Seismic and concept | Logged pay in TEC-6 and TEC-9, 175 m away |
| Facies control at target | None — stepping into undrilled rock | Bracketed by two wells that produced ~899 mbbl and ~353 mbbl |
| Completion | Selective stimulation in an open lateral | Conventional perforated interval |

TEC-11 established that El Abra quality degrades laterally away from the producing area. That finding *raises* the value of drilling into logged pay between two prolific wells. TEC-12 is the well least exposed to the risk TEC-11 discovered.

The honest framing for the incoming team: TEC-11 was a step-out that tested new rock and found it tight. That is exploration risk, correctly taken and correctly answered. TEC-12 carries development risk against direct well control, and the two should not be conflated.

## 6. TEC-12 opportunity analysis

### 6.1 Concept and target

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Target | Upper El Abra, -2,294 to -2,311 mSS |
| Planned TD | 2,370 mMD / 2,352.6 mTVD |
| Design | Directional from existing TEC-10 pad |
| Displacement | 175.2 m total — 35.1 m north, 171.6 m east, azimuth 078.4° |
| Target duration | 14 days drill and complete (20 days including pre-spud) |
| AFE | **$1,567,724** |

### 6.2 Well design

**Casing and completion (Build & Hold sketch):**

| Element | Specification | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| Conductor | 13⅜" J-55, 54.5 lb/ft, ID 12.615", drift 12.459" | 30 m |
| Surface | 9⅝" H-40, 32.3 lb/ft, ID 9.001", drift 8.845" | 500 m |
| Production | 7" L-80, 26 lb/ft, ID 6.276", drift 6.151" | 2,350 m |
| Tubing | 2⅞" L-80, 6.5 lb/ft with hydraulic packer, ID 2.441" | ~2,200 m |

**Deviation plan (S-shape) — a deliberately conservative trajectory:**

- Vertical to 570 m.
- Build at **1°/30 m** from 600 m to a maximum of **14–15°** by ~990 m.
- Hold ~14° to ~1,290 m.
- Drop at 1°/30 m, back to vertical by 1,710 m.
- **Vertical through the entire reservoir section**, 1,710 m to TD.

Two features deserve emphasis in the handover. Maximum dogleg severity is 1°/30 m and maximum inclination 15° — about as benign as a directional well gets. And the well is vertical through all of El Abra, which gives clean log response, a conventional perforated completion, and none of the liner-hanger and selective-stimulation complexity that defeated TEC-11 at 61° in a horizontal leg. The contrast with TEC-11 is the strongest single argument in the TEC-12 case.

The work program notes the directional plan was still under discussion — either a TEC-10-style build to the northeast, or a smooth TEC-9-like S-shape. **The S-shape is the option with an actual survey listing behind it.**

Mud: WBM 1.2–1.25 g/cc, or invert — undecided. Contingency 5" liner if hole problems arise (TEC-2, TEC-6 and TEC-7 ran a liner; TEC-9 did not).

### 6.5 Economics — and the overhead problem

Simmons scenarios, on the GLJ July 2020 WTI forecast adjusted for the Tonalli price formula, TEC-12 on stream March 2021 and TEC-13 January 2022, Simmons funding 100% of TEC-12 and 50% of TEC-13 for 40% before payout and 20% after:

| Case | Rate | Payout | 8-yr sales | 8-yr NOI | 8-yr EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 120 bbl/d | 7.8 yrs | 390,420 bbl | $5.57 MM | **-$4.97 MM** |
| Most likely | 170 bbl/d | 3.7 yrs | 504,274 bbl | $8.59 MM | **-$2.57 MM** |
| Upper likely | 235 bbl/d | 2.3 yrs | 651,664 bbl | $12.49 MM | **+$0.53 MM** |
| High | 300 bbl/d | 1.8 yrs | 799,323 bbl | $16.41 MM | **+$3.66 MM** |

Fiscal and cost structure across all cases:
- **Royalty 40%** — $14.31 to $21.11 per bbl.
- Revenue per bbl $35.85 (2021) rising to $52.18 (2028).
- Netback per bbl $9.21 to $21.93 depending on case and year.
- Operating costs largely fixed: $449,091 in 2021, then **$605,179 per year regardless of volume**.

**The finding that reframes the whole opportunity.** Below net operating income sit three fixed charges:

| Charge | Annual | 8-year total |
|---|---|---|
| IFR corporate | $550,000–660,000 | $5,170,000 |
| Tonalli G&A (salaries, insurance, audits, other) | ~$365,000 | $2,921,721 |
| Regulatory | ~$29,000 | $231,344 |
| **Total** | **~$1.05 MM/yr** | **$8,323,065** |

In the most-likely case the asset generates $8.59 MM of net operating income over eight years and carries $8.32 MM of corporate and regulatory overhead — roughly 97% of NOI consumed before the Simmons carry is even applied. That is what produces negative EBITDA at 170 bbl/d despite a 3.7-year well payout.

The conclusion is unambiguous and should be stated plainly to the incoming operator: **the TEC-12 well economics were never the problem. A single-asset listed company's cost structure was.** An acquirer with an existing Mexico platform absorbs most of that $8.3 MM, and the same well at the same rates looks entirely different. This is also the honest, verifiable answer to the question of why a well this well-defined was designed three times and never drilled.

Both the price deck (July 2020 GLJ) and the cost basis (November 2020) are now stale, and the fiscal regime changed under the March 2025 reform. A rebuild is required.

### 6.6 Risks

| Risk | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Karsting → premature water** | Regional karsting on the reef top improves reservoir quality but risks sinkhole topography and fracturing that connects to the aquifer. Precedent at TEC-101. Petrel Robertson: no significant karst signature on seismic at or near TEC-12. |
| **Fault-conduit water influx** | No evidence of large-scale faulting on seismic at or near the location. |
| **Depth prognosis error** | The 2011/12 PEMEX 3D was not designed for the shallow El Abra play; TEC-10 came in low. Seismic reprocessed and depth-migrated, but frequency tuning remains an issue. Mitigated by placing TEC-12 as close to TEC-9 as possible. |
| **Water cut** | Every well waters out cyclically. Not prohibitive — TEC-10 produces commercially at 30% oil cut — but it sets the economic limit. |
| **Facies risk** | Low relative to TEC-11 given bracketing well control, but not zero. See Section 5. |
| **Overhead** | The dominant historical risk. Materially reduced for an acquirer with an existing platform. |

**Mitigation recommended in source:** run FMI in TEC-12 as was done in TEC-10, to address fracture risk directly.

## 9. What the presentation should say

Suggested spine, in order:

1. **The rock has delivered.** ~2 MMbbl from four wells since 1956, pressure maintained at original by a strong aquifer, 1.2 MMbbl remaining at trend-average recovery.
2. **TEC-10 proved modern drilling works** — commercial production from 2.5 m of perforated pay, low on structure.
3. **TEC-11 failed, and here is exactly why** — the mud log facies column against the trajectory, showing ~900 m of lateral in tight mudstone–wackestone with one moderate show. Exploration risk on a step-out, correctly taken and correctly answered.
4. **TEC-12 is the opposite well in every respect that matters** — vertical through reservoir at 15° maximum inclination, bracketed by two prolific producers, 15 m of pay 16.5 m higher on structure, conventional completion, existing pad and gathering line.
5. **The economics never failed at the well level.** Payout of 1.8 to 3.7 years across the central cases; what failed was $8.3 MM of single-asset corporate overhead against $8.6 MM of net operating income.
6. **What we do not know, and the cheap tests that close it** — led by the static gradient on reopening.
