GENERAL

The Tecolutla property is located approximately 1.5 kilometres southwest from the coast of the Gulf of Mexico in Mexico, between the coordinates 97°0'0"W & 97°2'30"W and 20°26'0"N & 20°28'0"N. International Frontier Resources Corp. (IFR) entered into a joint venture with Grupo IDESA S.A. de C.V., named Tonalli Energía S.A.P.I. de C.V. (Tonalli), which was awarded 100 percent working interest in the Tecolutla Block in 2018. IFR holds a 50 percent working interest in Tonalli. A summary of interests and royalty burdens is included in the Land section of this report. Interest lands are illustrated on Map 1. Total property production forecast plots are provided as Plots 1 and 2. Individual reserves entity well production forecast plots are appended.

GEOLOGY

The Tecolutla Field is located in the Tampico embayment along the Golden Lane Reef trend. The fields in the Golden Lane Reef trend produce from the Middle Cretaceous aged El Abra Limestone. The El Abra Limestone was deposited in a shallow water shelf or lagoon environment. Limestone reefs of the El Abra Formation are composed of bioclastics, rudists and micritic-planktonic limestone. Coogan A. H., et. al., 19721 is an excellent geological overview of the El Abra Trend and producing fields in the Golden Lane and neighboring Poza Rica trend.

A PETRELTM geological model was provided to GLJ Ltd. (GLJ). GLJ reviewed the seismic interpretation for the Tecoluta Field and tied the structure maps provided to wells’ tops picked from well logs. Due to the age and quality of the of the well log data, only two wells were utilized to petrophysically calculate porosity (Tecolutla-9 and Tecolutla-10). As there is uncertainty in the Field due to lack of data, a low case average porosity and high case average porosity were calculated at 5 and 7 percent, respectively. An average of the low and high cases was used for the best case porosity estimate. Initial water saturation was estimated using the Tecolutla-9 well based on the Simandoux equation with a, m and n parameters of 1, 2 and 2, respectively, and a water resistivity value of 0.135 @ 25°C. A water saturation of 30 percent was calculated which was consistent with the Tonalli’s interpretation. The net to gross ratio over the Field was determined both from petrophysically evaluated wells, as well as estimated from microlog and neutron count logs in the older wells. A net to gross ratio of the Field was estimated to be at 40 percent.

GLJ used depth structure mapping of the top of the El Abra (Map 2) to calculate gross oil volume over the Tecolutla Field. For the low and high case gross oil volume (Map 3 & 4), an oil-water contact (OWC) of -2,374.2 metres was used, being defined by lowest known oil in the Tecoutla-3 well. For the high case gross oil volume (Map 4), a volume over the whole property was used. Due to the poor results from the Tecolutla-11 well, and the uncertainty to the source of the water, the low case gross oil included only volumes south of the Tecolutla-11 well (Map 3). An average of the gross oil volume from the low case and high case mapping was used for the best case volumes.

RESERVES AND ECONOMICS

Before being awarded to Tonalli, the Tecolutla Block in 2018, the property had seven wells drilled between 1956 and 1976 which produced 1,443 Mbbl of oil. Since the acquisition, Tonalli has drilled two wells, both in 2018. The Tecolutla-10 well came on production at approximately 160 bopd and is assigned recoverable volume of 300 Mbbl in the proved plus probable case. The Tecolutla-11 HZ well was drilled in 2018 and tested in 2019. The well was invaded with water and did not transition to oil as Tecolutla-10 did.

Tonalli has identified two additional drilling locations; Tecolutla-12 DIR and Tecolutla-13 HZ, scheduled to be drilled in 2021 and 2022, respectively. Each drilling location was assigned a volume of 400 Mbbl in the probable case and 600 Mstb in the possible case, with a proved volume of 250 Mbbl added in the Tecolutla-12 DIR based on offsetting production and existing surface pad synergies. A summary of oil reservoir and decline parameters is provided in Tables 2.1 and 2.2.

A summary of the economic parameters used in this evaluation, including product price adjustments, operating costs, and capital costs, is provided as Table 4. Operating costs and price adjustments are estimated from 2020 lease operating statements provided by the Operator. They also provided capital cost estimates which were deemed reasonable and used within this evaluation. Economic forecasts for the various reserves categories are included in the Economic Forecasts section.

Royalty burdens are a combination of a fixed amount and a sliding scale royalty dependent on the reference price received. The royalties have been estimated and applied the evaluation on an annualized basis.

Other Economic Considerations

• Non-reserves well abandonment and associated wellsite reclamation and facility abandonment/salvage including possible environmental concerns.

• Potential processing income.

• The current condition of field, gathering and processing facilities, i.e. an inspection was not carried out.
